How the calculation works

The calculator finds the volume of one pour, adds your waste allowance, converts it to the unit you order in, and divides by the yield of one bag.

  • Slab: length × width × thickness.
  • Round pad: π × (diameter ÷ 2)² × thickness.
  • Round column: π × (diameter ÷ 2)² × height.
  • Cubic yards: cubic feet ÷ 27.
  • Bags: volume with waste ÷ yield per bag, rounded up to a whole bag.

Worked example

A 10 ft × 10 ft slab, 4 in thick, is 10 × 10 × (4 ÷ 12) = 33.33 cubic feet. With a 10% allowance that is 36.67 cubic feet, or 1.36 cubic yards. A typical 80 lb bag yields about 0.6 cubic feet, so you need 62 bags.

Typical bag yields

Yields vary by brand, so use the figure on your bag. Approximate values used as defaults: 40 lb about 0.30 cu ft, 60 lb about 0.45 cu ft, 80 lb about 0.60 cu ft; 20 kg about 0.0094 m³, 25 kg about 0.0117 m³, 40 kg about 0.0187 m³.

Why add a waste allowance?

Uneven ground, spillage and slightly over-thick forms all use more concrete than the drawing says. Ten percent is a common starting point; use more for rough or uneven sub-bases.
